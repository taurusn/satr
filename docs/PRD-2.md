## 1. Summary

Linen 2 reads everything an agent hands Hatim. A note opens as it does today. A board opens on the weave: sections, cards, stickies and connectors at any zoom, labels that stay legible when zoomed out, hover notes with sources, an outline of sections, find that flies to each match, change marks on the boxes that changed since he last looked, a List view, and a reference to any box he can hand an agent. A page opens sealed: its scripts run when its folder is trusted, it cannot reach the network or a file outside its folder by any way Linen knows and tests, and Linen hands it the Loom look and the fonts so the file no longer carries them. On the Mac, boards export to a vector PDF or a PNG, notes and pages to PDF, notes to one self-contained HTML file. On the iPhone, Linen shows the same files, drawn by the same renderer, from a copy the Mac keeps up to date whenever the app is open near it.

**What Linen 2 does not promise.** It does not edit: boards open in Obsidian to edit (one key, as notes do). It does not sync through a cloud, reach the Mac from away from it, or reach a sleeping Mac: the phone gets changes while its app is open near the awake Mac, or when iOS lets it refresh in the background and the Mac is awake and reachable. Boards export to PDF and PNG only (HTML for boards is 2.x, because the fonts cannot travel). It does not run Obsidian plugins, render Excalidraw or tldraw files, or import FigJam's own files (closed format). The phone does not edit, pin or export at launch; it shares the original file. A sealed page is tested isolation, not a guarantee: Linen blocks every way out it knows and tests them after every system update, and runs page scripts by default only in folders Hatim trusts (PAGE-7, PAGE-10).

## 2. Who it is for and the jobs

Linen 1's five jobs stand (vault reader, reviewer, presenter, skimmer, keeper). Linen 2 adds:

6. **The board reviewer.** An agent writes or updates a board (a readiness map of about 170 boxes in ten lanes, an architecture diagram). Hatim opens it, sees which boxes changed since he last looked, steps through them, hovers a box for its evidence, and points the agent at an exact box by copying its reference.
7. **The couch reader.** At home or in a meeting room, on the iPhone, he opens the plan or the map the agent finished. It arrived the last time the app was open near the Mac, and it shows what changed.
8. **The board presenter** (2.x). He shows a board on the TV or in a meeting: Present walks its sections in order with smooth moves. At launch he presents by stepping through sections from the outline.
9. **The sender.** He sends a board to someone who has none of his apps: a PDF that looks like Loom's paper with its sources at the end, or a PNG for a chat.
10. **The agent author.** An agent writes a board, a note or a page, checks it, lays it out, takes its own screenshot at phone and laptop widths in both looks to verify it, and opens it in Linen. No browser in the loop.

**What users complain about** (744 recent App Store reviews of Figma, Freeform and Miro, counted by script): crashes and lag (Miro 30, Figma 21, Freeform 10), lost work (Miro 18), zoom and legibility (Miro 17), view-only phone apps (Figma 28), big boards that will not load on a phone ("Every single board says it's “too large” and won't load"), and needing the internet. **What this means:** no board app documents a vector PDF with selectable text, reading-first zoom, per-block Arabic in an open format and an offline phone reader together. A read-only phone app is acceptable only if it says so plainly, opens big boards fast and never loses anything (BRD-20, BRD-23, BRD-24, SYNC-4).

**On the iPhone** (`docs/research/iphone-sync.md` section 6, App Store data 2026-10-14): Read.md is the closest reader (free with a Pro tier, offline, Mermaid, files from Files and AirDrop, no sync); Obsidian reads through its paid Sync or iCloud; 1Writer, Textastic and Documents move files through a cloud, WebDAV or a manual Wi-Fi transfer; Freeform edits boards through iCloud; LocalSend and Möbius Sync (Syncthing) move files Mac to phone without a cloud but render nothing. None of the apps saved reads JSON Canvas on the phone. Linen 2's place: files from your own Mac, over your own network, no cloud and no account, kept up to date by itself, read offline with Arabic per block, notes, boards and sealed pages in one reader.

## 4. Goals, non-goals and measures

**Goals**
- Every page and diagram an agent makes for Hatim opens in Linen, never in a browser, on both devices.
- A board reads well at every zoom, on a 13-inch laptop and on a 6-inch phone.
- For notes and boards, the agent writes content only; the look, the fonts, the layout and the phone version come from Linen. A page keeps its own layout: Linen gives it the look and fonts (PAGE-3), and the agent checks its phone version with `linen shot --width 390`.
- Nothing leaves Hatim's two devices: no cloud, no account, no network from documents. The one way out of a page is a web link Hatim chooses to open in his browser after seeing its full address (PAGE-11).

**Non-goals**
- Editing notes, boards or pages; a cloud; reaching the Mac from away from it (a relay is Later); Windows, Android or the web; collaboration; importing FigJam, Miro, Excalidraw or tldraw files; plugins.

**Measures.** MacBook Air M4 (macOS 26.5) and iPhone 15 Pro (iOS 26.5), release builds, 20 runs, p50 and p95, signposts. "Board first paint" means every node inside the Canvas view drawn in final fonts at its level; the benchmark switches to Canvas and opens the first section at reading zoom with real cards (Markdown, sanitising, Thmanyah, note threads), not shapes, even where BRD-24 would open List view first. "List first paint" is measured on its own: the first screen of the List view of the same board in final fonts. Frame measures report the share of dropped frames (over 25 ms), the p99 and the longest hitch, the delay from touch to the first moved frame, and memory; a jetsam or a WebContent crash fails the measure.

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Open a 500-node, 700-edge board to first paint | Mac p95 under 400 ms; iPhone p95 under 800 ms |
| Open a 2,000-node, 3,000-edge board to first paint | Mac p95 under 1.2 s; iPhone p95 under 2 s |
| Open the 500-node board in List view to List first paint | iPhone p95 under 500 ms |

| Pan and zoom on the 2,000-node board | Mac and iPhone: p95 frame under 25 ms, under 3 percent dropped; iPhone web content memory under 300 MB |
| Reload of the 500-node board after an outside save | repainted within 250 ms of the last write; the node at the view's centre within 4 px of where it was |
| `linen layout` on the reference board (about 170 nodes) | under 2 s, every text fitting its card |
| A pinned 60 KB note changed on the Mac, phone app open, same Wi-Fi | changed on the phone within 2 s; over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi within 3 s |
| First sync of 100 MB of pinned files, app open | under 60 s on home Wi-Fi |

## 5. Principles

Linen 1's six principles stand. Linen 2 adds:

7. **The agent writes content; Linen draws.** Fonts, tokens, layout, sizes, the phone version and the export come from Linen, never from the file.
8. **A file is the truth.** Boards and pages are plain files an agent can write, diff and fix. No database and no cloud; the phone holds copies.
9. **Legible at every zoom.** A board never shows unreadable grey at overview: sections and titles keep a readable size, text that would fall under 9 px is not drawn, a box's state shows as its shape, and detail returns as you zoom in. On the phone a board can also be read as a list.
10. **Sealed, and honest about it.** A page keeps its scripts and interactions inside walls tested against every way out Linen knows, retested after every system update. Linen calls this tested isolation, never a guarantee, and runs scripts by default only where Hatim trusts the author.
11. **One place per signal, on every surface.** Sync state lives in one line on the phone and one row in Settings on the Mac; a box's state is one status word.

### 6.2 Pages (PAGE)

| ID | Requirement | Tier |

## 8. Design

Linen 1's section 8 stands. For boards:

- **Ground:** the weave (Loom's dot grid at `--grid`), which Linen 1 kept off reading pages; on a board it is the canvas itself, and it scales with zoom up to a density where it fades out. Night: the suite's weave at 9 percent.
- **Cards:** raised paper (paper, 1 px rule, the soft ink shadow), 2 px corners, padding 14 px; title Thmanyah Serif Display Light 19 (Loom's card title), body Sans 15 (Loom's body, board text is read in short pieces); status as an eyebrow pill (one per card).
- **Stickies:** a square wash, no border and no shadow, Sans 15 at weight 500 for the first line.
- **Colours on a board:** JSON Canvas's six preset colours and any hex colour are drawn as flat Loom washes built only from the tokens with alpha (no new hues), told apart by lightness and named on hover; never with threads, dots or dashes (those are the status shapes' language, WORDS-2) and never with rust (failure only). *A departure from FigJam's bright stickies, chosen by Hatim on 2026-10-14 (open question 1).*
- **Sections:** a tint-1 wash at low alpha with a rule border; label as an eyebrow; title in serif 24.
- **Connectors:** ink-2, 1.5 px, small open arrowheads at the `toEnd` only unless the file says otherwise (no implied two-way arrows); labels on paper chips in Sans 12.5.
- **Status shapes** (WORDS-2): drawn in ink, so Night inverts them with the ink token (light lines on the dark ground) and the quietest state stays the quietest; a box's fill never carries a state by tint alone. Threads (Partial) stop behind a box's title: the title sits on a paper band, so hatching never crosses letters (the redrawn sketch showed map-level titles cut by the lines). Contrast of each shape against paper and against a section's wash is checked by the token lint in both looks.
- **Arabic boards** (BRD-27): section names and change threads at the top-right corner, note cards preferring the left side, the outline and List view right-aligned; numbers stay in the file's digits.
- **Small text:** at rest, text that would draw under 9 px is not drawn (titles become the card's shape, section names drop their eyebrow, then shrink to their 15 px floor or give way to a neighbour).
- **Floating chrome over a board** (the zoom panel with its legend, the change chip) is glass, as Loom's floating panels; note cards over text stay solid paper (Linen 1's rule).
- **Phone:** the same tokens; every touch target 44 pt, the Canvas / List switch included; the outline, notes and settings as sheets.
- **Digits and paths:** one digit system per screen (Arabic-Indic throughout an Arabic interface, counts and zoom included); paths and file names keep their own case and are never set as uppercase eyebrows.
- **Toasts** (Copy Reference and the like) are solid paper, as note cards are, since they sit over card text.

## 9. Architecture

### 9.1 The board format

**JSON Canvas 1.0** (`docs/research/boards.md` section 1), with Linen's own fields under one `linen` object on the canvas, on each node and on each edge: kind, status word, note, tour steps, layout hints, connector kind. The spec says nothing about unknown fields (issues #8, #13 and #57 are open with no maintainer answer), so the guide is Obsidian's own code: version 1.13.7 keeps unknown keys at all three levels when it saves (read from its app archive, not yet run; section 13 adds a round trip). Four rules follow:

1. **Only the four spec types.** Obsidian drops any node whose `type` is not `text`, `file`, `link` or `group`. Card, sticky, shape and text are `"type": "text"` with `linen.kind`; note, board, page and image are `"type": "file"`, told apart by extension; link is `"type": "link"`; sections are groups. `linen check` rejects any other `type`.
2. **No dangling edges.** Obsidian drops an edge whose end node is missing, so `linen check` reports one as an error; the gap mark (BRD-4) stays for files that arrive broken.
3. **Missing sides by Obsidian's rule.** Obsidian fills a missing `fromSide` or `toSide` from the angle between the two nodes' centres and writes it; Linen uses the same rule, so a board keeps its shape after a save in Obsidian.
4. **Obsidian's file style.** `linen layout` writes whole-number positions and sizes, tab indents, flat objects on one line and no undefined keys, and after its first normalisation patches only the numbers it changes (CLI-3), so a round trip through Obsidian makes a small diff.
5. **Always valid.** Every node carries its position and size, as the spec requires, including inside laid-out sections; agents write approximate positions or run `linen layout` before handing a board over (BRD-1, BRD-17).

If a future Obsidian dropped unknown keys, the fallback is a sidecar `map.canvas.linen.json` keyed by node and edge `id`, the one thing every save keeps. Notes may live in the board or in `map.canvas.notes.json` (NOTE-2).

### 9.2 Renderer

- **Boards render in the same page** as notes (a second mode of `Renderer/page.js`), so cards reuse the Markdown pipeline, sanitising, Arabic rules, Mermaid and KaTeX. The board is DOM and SVG: nodes are absolutely placed elements inside one transformed layer, connectors one SVG layer under them. Only nodes near the view are in the DOM (BRD-20).
- **Four rendering rules from spike B(a)** (`docs/spikes/boards/README.md`). In the iPhone simulator on the M4, with plain HTML cards in system fonts: at 500 cards every gesture held a p95 of 17 ms with 1 or 2 dropped frames; at 2,000 cards zoom p95 fell from 881 ms (v1) to 22 ms, with 2 to 6 dropped frames per 180. The 16 to 46 ms first paints were taken at Fit, which at those sizes is the shapes canvas, not cards. Real cards (Markdown, sanitising, Thmanyah, note threads) at reading zoom, the Mac and a real iPhone are still unmeasured (S2 gate). The rules: (1) mid-gesture only the transform changes; (2) cards in view that are not mounted are drawn as their status shapes on one canvas, which is also the whole board at the areas level; (3) at rest cards mount within a per-frame budget, nearest the centre first; (4) the board layer is composited only while cards are mounted above the areas level, and crossing into the areas level mid-gesture hides the mounted cards at once.
- **Work that does not depend on animation frames.** WebKit nearly stops animation frames in hidden and offscreen windows (Linen 1's build log), so the mount budget and layout yield through a message channel when the view is hidden, as Linen 1's render slices do; otherwise `linen shot` and a background tab would stall.
- **Connectors** are culled like nodes, through a spatial index of their bounding boxes, so a 3,000-edge board mounts only the edges near the view.
- **Layout:** a layout module in the renderer, the same code for the app and the command. Lanes, grid and stack are Linen's own code; *layered* sections and nodes without positions use **ELK.js 0.12** (`docs/research/boards.md` section 4: the only open engine with layered layout, ports, nested groups and orthogonal routing together; in JavaScriptCore one 18-node section takes 6 ms at p50, a 176-node board 74 ms, 2,000 nodes 1.4 s). ELK runs in a Web Worker, loads only when a board asks for a layout (about 37 ms to load and start), and its result is cached by content hash. Sections are laid out one at a time inside their hand-placed frames (one global layout put ten sections in a single row 19,988 px wide). Hand-placed nodes never go to ELK, which cannot pin coordinates; routing around cards across a hand-placed board (Linen's own small orthogonal router) is 2.x. `elk.bundled.js` ships unmodified as its own file with its EPL-2.0 text; dagre (MIT, 16 KB) is the fallback if size ever matters more than routing.
- **Pages** use a second `WKWebView` configuration, its own non-persistent data store and so its own web content process:
  - **Origins.** Each opened page gets its own unguessable origin, `linen-page://<random per open>/`, so two pages never share storage or reach each other. WebKit registers a handler per scheme, not per host, so the handler itself answers only the tab's own host and refuses every other authority. The kit is a separate origin, `linen-kit://kit/`, serving an exact list of files (PAGE-3). Every response carries `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. P(a) tested a scheme-wide rule, not per-open hosts or `'self'` on a custom scheme; spike P(c) tests them before M9.
  - **The CSP header**, sent by the scheme handler with the page, literally: `default-src 'none'; script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline' 'unsafe-eval' blob:; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline' linen-kit:; img-src 'self' data: blob:; font-src 'self' data: linen-kit:; media-src 'self' data: blob:; worker-src 'self' blob:; connect-src 'self'; frame-src 'self'; child-src 'self' blob:; object-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'; manifest-src 'none'`, with `script-src` replaced by `'none'` when PAGE-7 turns scripts off. No script comes from the kit: its behaviour is injected in an isolated world (PAGE-3). `connect-src 'self'` lets a page read files from its own folder (PAGE-2) and nothing else. P(c) also tests adding `sandbox allow-scripts`, whose opaque origin changes what `'self'` matches, removes storage, and may break sibling files and kit fonts.
  - **The content rule list** blocks everything, then lets `linen-page:` (whose hosts the handler polices), `linen-kit:`, `about:`, `blob:` and `data:` through (P(a)).
  - **The removal script** (P(b)) is a `WKUserScript` at document start in all frames, deleting `RTCPeerConnection`, the other `RTC*` constructors and `WebTransport` and pinning them to `undefined`. It removes known APIs, a blocklist; PAGE-10's per-build inventory is what catches APIs nobody knows yet, and fails closed on a WebKit build Linen has not reviewed.
  - **Delegates** allow navigation only within the page's origin and `about:blank` and `about:srcdoc`, refuse popups, downloads and every capability (PAGE-11), and route real clicks reported by the isolated-world listener (PAGE-8) through PAGE-11's link allowlist, never through SAFE-4's general routes.
  - **Every scriptable response carries the CSP.** The handler sends the header with every HTML document and every script it serves (pages, frames, worker and worklet scripts), not only the top page; blob workers inherit it. The removal script reaches frames, not worker globals, so workers rely on the CSP and the rule list, which P(c) tests realm by realm.
  - Lockdown mode was measured as an alternative and rejected as the default (page scripts about 40 times slower, no WebAssembly or WebGL).
- **The command** is a small executable inside Linen.app that hosts the renderer in its own offscreen `WKWebView`, the way the harness does today, with CLI-4's deadline and crash stop.

### 9.3 The link

From `docs/research/iphone-sync.md` sections 1 to 4 and 7. Everything below works on the free Apple team: Bonjour and local network need only Info.plist keys, peer-to-peer Wi-Fi is one parameter, and TLS and the keychain need no capability.

- **Trust: TLS 1.3 with a self-signed certificate on each side, each pinned by its SHA-256 at pairing.** Measured on loopback: TLS 1.3 ready in 10 to 17 ms; a wrong pin, an impostor on either side and a phone with no certificate all fail. Each device makes its key once (`SecKeyCreateRandomKey`, kept in its own keychain, this device only), builds a self-signed certificate and an identity (`SecIdentityCreate`, public on both systems); no PKCS#12 and no paid team. Rejected: a pre-shared key from the QR (Network.framework refuses TLS 1.3 with a PSK, and in TLS 1.2 the QR would be the lasting secret, open to anyone who photographs it), and a Noise handshake of Linen's own (custom cryptography outside Apple's stack). There is no TLS 1.2 fallback: if client certificates misbehave on the iPhone in spike L(a), the design comes back to review rather than downgrading.
- **Pairing mode (SYNC-1).** Both sides cannot be pinned in the first handshake, because the Mac does not know the phone's certificate yet, so pairing is a separate, short mode:
  1. Hatim opens Settings > iPhone > Pair. The helper makes a random 128-bit code and shows a QR with the Mac's certificate hash, its Bonjour instance name and the code, valid for 2 minutes. While the QR is up, and only then, the listener accepts a TLS session from an unknown phone certificate, one at a time.
  2. The phone scans the QR (that tap is when iOS asks for local network access, never at launch), dials, and pins the Mac by the hash from the QR, so only the real Mac can complete the session. Its own self-signed certificate goes in the same handshake.
  3. Inside the session the phone sends the code. A wrong code counts; three end pairing mode and the QR is discarded.
  4. Both sides derive six digits from TLS keying material exported from that session plus both certificate hashes, so the digits bind this exact session; both screens show them, and Hatim clicks Allow on the Mac only if they match.
  5. Only after Allow does the Mac pin the phone's certificate and close pairing mode. Any other unknown certificate is refused, in pairing mode and out of it.
  6. **A pairing session can only pair.** On a session from an unpinned certificate the Mac runs a separate state machine with exactly two application frames. The phone sends one `pair-code` frame (the code from the QR, its device name). Each side computes the six digits itself from the session; they are never sent. The phone shows them with "Confirm on your Mac"; the Mac shows them with Allow and Refuse. When Hatim answers, or after 60 s, the Mac sends one `pair-result` frame (`allowed` or `refused`, and a reason: `code`, `expired`, `refused`, `timeout`, `busy`) and closes. Any other frame, a second `pair-code`, or anything after `pair-result` ends pairing. "No data sent" is exact: on a pairing session the Mac sends no application frame but `pair-result`. Sync always starts on a new session in which both certificates are already pinned.
  A photo of the QR is useless after 2 minutes or after one pairing. A phone that is forgotten, or that makes a new key, pairs again.
- **Who dials:** the helper advertises `_linen._tcp` and listens; the phone browses and dials, with `includePeerToPeer` on. Every route Bonjour reports is tried at once and the first secure session wins (SYNC-6). Bonjour over the USB cable's network link was seen working on Hatim's iPhone in an earlier project (2026-10-13: found over the cable, 155 ms to connected, and back on peer-to-peer Wi-Fi 0.43 s after unplugging); Apple documents none of it, so M12 tests it again and it stays a bonus route.
- **Protocol** (SYNC-4), framed with `NWProtocolFramer` as Apple's peer-to-peer sample is. Frames on a pinned session, Mac (M) or phone (P):
  - `hello` (P): protocol version, the pin-set generation it holds, and per pin the manifest ID and sequence it holds.
  - `pins` (M, when the phone's pin-set generation is older): the new pin-set generation number and the whole pin set, each pin's ID, kind (`folder` or `file`), name and trust revision; a pin absent from the list is removed, with its files. `pins-ok` (P) names the generation number it has applied; the phone stores that number only after the additions and removals are done, and reports it in its next `hello`.
  - `manifest` (M, per pin): its manifest ID and trust revision, then entries newer than the phone's sequence: relative path, size, modification time, SHA-256, sequence, the page's effective script policy (`on` or `off`) and its source (`root:<id>`, `file`, or `untrusted`), and each note's resolved link targets as (pin ID, relative path); removals as tombstones. A trust change raises the trust revision and re-sends the affected entries even when no file changed.
  - `want` (P): hashes with resume offsets, after the phone's space check (SYNC-8).
  - `chunk` (M): hash, offset, bytes. `done` (P): a generation switched, with its sequence.
  - `changed` (M, while connected): a pin and its new sequence or trust revision, or a new pin-set generation.
  Any frame not in this list, or in the wrong direction, closes the session. From Syncthing's protocol: the delta manifest by ID and sequence, whole-file hashes (no block hashes at first) and temporary files; from LocalSend's: metadata before bytes and a hash check on arrival. The phone writes into a `.partial` file named by the expected hash, resumes from its length and checks the hash; the whole pin's generation then switches at once (SYNC-4). Left out: version vectors and conflicts (the phone never writes), block reuse, multicast discovery.
- **Paths and bounds.** The phone never sends a path, only hashes it wants; the Mac never serves a file outside a pin's root, walking it with SAFE-2's descriptor rule: a folder pin's root is its folder; a single-file pin's root is that one file, opened from its own file-scoped grant, and its walk may resolve to that file only. Manifest paths are relative, NFC, and validated component by component on both sides: no empty or `.` or `..` components, no absolute paths, no symlinks created on the phone; two paths that differ only by case or Unicode form are refused with a problem line. Every frame is bounded (a manifest page under 1 MB, a chunk under 1 MB, at most 50,000 entries per pin), and anything larger closes the session.
- **The helper's reach.** The helper runs in the app sandbox with one security-scoped grant per pin, folder-scoped for a folder pin and file-scoped for a single-file pin, passed by the app when Hatim pins, and nothing else; the app, the helper and the `linen` command talk over XPC, and each side checks the other's code signature from its audit token.
- **Local network privacy.** iOS asks once; when denied, Bonjour finds nothing and connections wait with `localNetworkDenied`, which Linen reads to show the right state (SYNC-7). On the Mac, registering the Bonjour service needs the privilege and accepting connections does not; macOS ties a helper's prompt to its app. WKWebView traffic is exempt from local network privacy, so it is no second wall for sealed pages (PAGE-1 does not rely on it).
- **iOS background.** Sync runs while the app is open. On leaving, the link asks `beginBackgroundTask` for time to reach a safe point (Apple gives no fixed time and no promise), keeps its partial files and stops; it resumes when the app returns (SYNC-5). `BGAppRefreshTask` (free, needs the `fetch` background mode) wakes the app when iOS chooses; it dials only an awake Mac it can reach, fetches within the time given and pauses the same way; a local network operation in the background is refused while the privilege is undetermined, which pairing has already settled. In 2.x, the first sync and "Sync now" may run as a `BGContinuedProcessingTask` (iOS 26) with a Live Activity.

## 11. Constraints and risks
